The GPS avoided a goose — and now the goose owns the road.

> I avoided the goose. The goose owns that road now. I do not argue with geese.

The line lands because the GPS speaks in its own deadpan first-person voice, not a narrator apologising: it made a deliberate choice, and the choice was surrender to one specific goose — an absurdly concrete obstacle a reader can picture instantly, not a vague map error. The joke is the excuse itself — a navigation computer ceding the road to a bird — in a three-beat rhythm that saves the punch ("I do not argue with geese") for the last words. No recalculating, no lost signal, no stale map: just a machine that knows when it is beaten, told in only 77 characters, so a short, tight line that is easy to remember and hard to beat on craft.
